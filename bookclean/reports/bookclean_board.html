The harm model — a mechanism, not a vibe

JUNK HARM  ∝  FREQUENCY  ×  SLOT-UNCONTESTEDNESS  ×  INCOMPRESSIBILITY

<DWnn> redaction tokenfails frequency + contestedness0 emission 271k tokens, years of runs
Furniture (###.)frequent + uncontested but compressible0 gap clean model reconstructs it
CIP / publisher boilerplatefrequent + uncontested + incompressible11× maxes all three axes

Three experiments — observational (redaction natural experiment), controlled (this ablation), arithmetic (slot competition) — each pinned one axis. Duplication was the harm axis all along.

✓ confirmed

Content control null (−0.004): the gap is junk-specific. Cross-book dedup (v10) removes the real magnets — value empirically confirmed.

✗ killed claim (Rook #216)

"Within-book magnets cause gradient anomalies / loss-spikes" — did NOT replicate (229 vs 211 outliers = nothing). Logged, with his name on it.

⚠ spec bug

The pre-reg threshold never defined the population — so 1.6× (aggregate) and 11× (worst-2%) both read legitimately. Lesson: pre-register the population, not just the threshold.

↺ reframe

The 20× cut-asymmetry is vindicated, not indicted: smaller junk-harm → leaving junk is cheaper → true asymmetry > 20× → "when in doubt, keep" now has empirical backing.

Reframe of v11: within-book dedup shows ~no training-harm value at this scale — kept as corpus hygiene (free, zero content damage), not memorization defense. Caveat: 130M / 0.63 epoch = a lower bound. Next: the multi-epoch × capacity scaling grid → predict the gap at wizard101's real 4.6B scale. Span judge: dead. Contingent successor: lower cross-book dup threshold 3→2 (the untouched 2-book bucket = 92% of dup lines).

Hard-won lessons

The things that cost time to learn — kept here so they don't have to be re-learned.

01 · Rules > LLMs for boundaries

Our engine and the independent OCR project reached this separately. Per-item neural classification loses the cross-item coherence a boundary needs.

02 · Knowing you're right is the hard part

v8 cut learnable prose for two days and every jury approved — because we wrote the rubric. An external, decorrelated view is what exposed it.

03 · The metric can reward doing nothing

Under a 20× eat-penalty, a filter that cuts 0% scores "great." Always report marginal-over-rules recall and content-eaten, never AUC alone.

04 · The ~25% ceiling is architectural

Single-cut recall is capped by non-monotone back matter (junk interleaved with prose). Not a classifier, gold, or rules problem — structural.

05 · Harm decomposition inverted the plan

Half the unreachable tail junk is near-zero-harm fluent prose; the dangerous part is duplicated boilerplate. So dedup comes first — it catches the magnets nearly free.

06 · Standing debt — PAID

The belief everything proxied — "junk hurts training" — was finally tested (Board 3). Verdict: real but modest (1.6×), and duplication was the harm axis all along. The harness survived contact with its own ground truth: two claims died in public, and the surviving conclusion is load-bearing.

Recipe today = de-redaction → structural boundary (v9) → cross-book dedup (v10) → within-book dedup (v11), all complementary, all CPU-cheap after the boundary engineering. v11 is corpus of record. The harm ablation is complete — cross-book dedup empirically confirmed, within-book reframed as hygiene, the span judge dead. Next: the multi-epoch × capacity scaling grid to read the memorization gap at production scale. Parked for v12 — exempt pure-syntax lines (|, ---|---) from the within-book circuit breaker so pipe-scaffolded classics get cleaned too.
